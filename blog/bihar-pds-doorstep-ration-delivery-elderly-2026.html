Bihar Will Deliver Ration to the Doorsteps of Citizens Above 75

Bihar will begin doorstep PDS ration delivery for people above 75 from next year, lowering the eligibility age from 80. What it means for CSR and elder care.

Orvys Research Team · 19 November 2026 · 5 min read

On 12 November, Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary announced that Bihar will begin doorstep delivery of PDS foodgrains to citizens above 75 years of age starting next year, lowering the eligibility threshold from the 80-plus pilot the state's food department had proposed earlier in 2026. The announcement came at a ration-card distribution event at Gyan Bhawan in Patna, alongside Deputy Chief Minister Vijay Kumar Choudhary and Food and Consumer Protection Minister Ashok Choudhary.

What the Bihar doorstep ration delivery scheme covers

Bihar has issued 2.09 crore ration cards, covering around 8.46 crore people across the state's Public Distribution System. Under the new commitment, citizens above 75 will no longer need to travel to their local fair price shop each month — foodgrains will instead be delivered directly to their homes. The age threshold is itself a sign of how fast this idea has moved: the Food and Consumer Protection Department first floated a pilot for citizens above 80 in May 2026, and within six months the government has both committed to a firm rollout and widened who qualifies. Speaking at the same event, Choudhary said "no poor person in Bihar should ever sleep hungry," tying the announcement to the state's broader ration-card and PDS-shop licensing drive.

Why this matters for CSR-funded elder care

Doorstep ration delivery solves access, not isolation or health monitoring — the two gaps where CSR-funded elder-care programmes typically add the most value alongside a state welfare scheme. A last-mile delivery visit is also a natural check-in point: the same frontline worker who drops off foodgrain can flag a beneficiary who needs a health check, a pension-linkage issue, or a referral to the kind of primary-care access we've covered in CSR and primary health centres. For companies running elder-care or last-mile welfare programmes in Bihar, a state-run doorstep channel reaching 75-plus citizens is infrastructure worth designing around rather than duplicating.

It also fits a pattern we've tracked through the year: Bihar's welfare delivery is shifting from camp-and-collect models toward doorstep and digital-first service, a trend that shapes where CSR convergence opportunities actually sit.
