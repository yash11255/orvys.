₹11,096 Crore for Power, a New MSME Policy: Bihar's Cabinet and CSR

Bihar's cabinet approved 43 proposals including a ₹11,096 crore power plan and MSME Policy 2026, targeting 1 crore entrepreneurial households.

Orvys Research Team · 16 November 2026 · 5 min read

On 30 October, the Bihar Cabinet, chaired by Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary, approved 43 proposals in a single sitting, including the Bihar MSME Policy 2026 — a mix of big infrastructure spending and quieter policy decisions that matter more for CSR planning than the headline numbers suggest.

Bihar cabinet decisions 2026: the big-ticket items

The largest approval was a ₹11,096 crore power distribution plan. Alongside it, the Cabinet sanctioned ₹18.21 crore for a feasibility study into an underground road corridor in Patna, and ₹12 crore to organise the Bihar State Games 2026. The Cabinet also granted ex-post facto approval to the Bihar MSME Policy 2026, and separately cleared restrictions on the purchase, sale and transfer of land earmarked for new greenfield satellite townships around the state — part of a wider push to expand urban infrastructure announced alongside the cabinet's other recent decisions.

Why Bihar MSME Policy 2026 is the one to watch

The power and road numbers will matter for contractors and utilities; the MSME Policy 2026 is the one with direct CSR relevance. Its stated aim is to turn one crore families in Bihar into entrepreneurial households, generating one crore direct and indirect jobs in the process. That's a state target of a scale that no single government scheme can deliver through subsidy and licensing reform alone — it needs skilling, mentorship, access-to-credit support and market linkages running alongside it, which is precisely the kind of work CSR budgets already fund under Schedule VII's provisions for skill development and livelihood enhancement.

For companies already running programmes in this space, the policy gives a concrete, state-endorsed target to align against rather than a generic "promote entrepreneurship" framing. We've covered the mechanics of this kind of programme design in CSR skilling and livelihood projects that lead to jobs, and the women's-enterprise angle — a large share of Bihar's self-help-group economy that any serious MSME push has to include — in our piece on CSR, women's empowerment and SHG enterprise. For the broader pattern of where CSR money actually lands in the state today, see CSR in Bihar: where the money goes.
