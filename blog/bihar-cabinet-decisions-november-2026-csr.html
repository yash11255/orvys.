32 Decisions, One Cabinet Meeting: What Bihar's Latest Announcements Mean for CSR

Bihar's Cabinet cleared 32 decisions on 10 November 2026, from a women's startup scheme to a new ITI and rural housing funds. A look at what it means for companies planning CSR in Bihar.

Orvys Research Team · 14 November 2026 · 6 min read

Ahead of Chhath Puja, Bihar's Cabinet, chaired by Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary, cleared 32 decisions on 10 November 2026, spanning education, employment, infrastructure, agriculture, technology and welfare. Several of them open direct convergence opportunities for companies running CSR programmes in the state.

The headline decisions

  • Dearness allowance hiked to 63% for state government employees and pensioners, effective from July.
  • Bihar Ki Beti Startup Challenge Scheme approved with ₹126 crore in funding to encourage entrepreneurship and research among female students, running across four financial years from 2026-27 to 2029-30, with digital infrastructure and technology tools for women-led startups and researchers.
  • A new Industrial Training Institute (ITI) approved at Basopatti in Madhubani district.
  • 159 prison calling kiosks cleared across 59 jails in the state.
  • Bihar Canal Maintenance Policy 2026 and revised building bye-laws also received Cabinet approval.

Rural housing funds, separately confirmed

Alongside the Cabinet round, the Centre and state also cleared funding for rural housing: a Central share of ₹450.15 crore and a State share of ₹949.45 crore to support homeless rural families building permanent houses, part of the broader PMAY-G push covered in our piece on PMAY-G's FY27 funding release.

Where CSR can converge

  • The new Basopatti ITI needs equipment, trainers and placement linkages before it can deliver outcomes, exactly the kind of skilling gap CSR funding regularly closes; see our guide on skilling projects that lead to jobs.
  • Bihar Ki Beti Startup Challenge beneficiaries are a natural fit for CSR-funded mentorship, incubation space or seed capital that complements, rather than duplicates, the state's own ₹126 crore commitment.
  • Rural housing allocations mean newly sanctioned PMAY-G beneficiaries in Bihar will need the last-mile support, solar lighting, water access, livelihood linkages, that government housing funds typically do not cover.

This is one Cabinet meeting in a state that has announced welfare and infrastructure decisions at an unusually fast pace this year. Companies planning CSR budgets for Bihar should track state announcements alongside central ones; see our broader guide on working with state governments on CSR convergence for how to build that into a programme design.
