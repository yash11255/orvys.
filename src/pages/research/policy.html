Strategic Context

The emerging policy architecture in Bihar

Bihar receives about ₹25 of CSR per person per year — against a national average of ₹223. The Government of Bihar's 2025 State CSR Policy aims to change that by creating an institutional mechanism to attract, guide and monitor corporate social investments across all 38 districts.

Three key governance instruments now shape the landscape: a Chief Secretary-led State CSR Society, a real-time tracking portal aimed at mobilising ₹5,000 crore, and institutional convergence dialogues with UNICEF and academic partners like CIMP Patna.

Tracked Actions

Key policy initiatives

PolicyJul 2025

Bihar State CSR Policy 2025

Government of Bihar · Statewide

Reported in July 2025 as Bihar's first State CSR Policy. A CSR Society oversees CSR activity under a Chief Secretary-led governing body and executive committee. Reported priority sectors: education, healthcare, rural infrastructure, skill development and women's empowerment. The report cites 24,932 CSR-eligible companies.

Orvys Perspective: Pair the policy with incentives and recognition for companies, publish district-wise CSR data every year, and set measurable targets for under-served districts.

PlatformAug 2025

Bihar CSR Portal

Government of Bihar · Statewide

A real-time CSR portal launched on 18 August 2025 by Deputy Chief Minister Samrat Choudhary, aiming to mobilise ₹5,000 crore 'over the next few years'. It covers a dashboard of CSR inflows by sector and district, NGO registration, a corporate interface and district needs uploaded by District Magistrates.

Orvys Perspective: Add outcome fields, not just spend; open the data for download; flag districts with low CSR per person; and verify NGO profiles against CSR-1 records.
Verified sources:
  1. The CSR Universe, 'Bihar launches real-time CSR portal' (18 Aug 2025)
ConveningDec 2025

Roundtable on State CSR Policy implementation

UNICEF and CIMP · Patna

Held in Patna during the 5th International Conference on CSR. Discussed flexible funding pathways, state and district institutional mechanisms, and a user-friendly portal for tracking projects and impact.

Orvys Perspective: Turn the roundtable into standing thematic working groups (WASH, nutrition, skilling) with published outputs and named district pilots.

Orvys Recommendations

How Bihar can unlock CSR scale

  • Standardize District CSR Project Banks: Provide pre-costed, Schedule VII-aligned DPRs across the 13 Aspirational Districts so corporates can fund projects without lengthy feasibility cycles.
  • Incentivize Under-Funded Geographies: Recognize corporate funders investing in districts receiving under ₹1 Cr/year through an annual state Governor's CSR citation.
  • Transparent NGO Verification: Connect the state portal directly with MCA CSR-1, 12A/80G, and Darpan registries to reduce due diligence bottlenecks.
  • Multi-Year Pooled Funds: Enable corporate unspent CSR funds to be deposited into multi-year ongoing projects managed through transparent Escrow accounts.
